## Changes (triage A)

1. **Closeness before `word_dropped`.** The order is now … `inflection`, `not_close`, `word_dropped`,
   `anchor_confident`. A function word counts as dropped only if most of its letters are deleted in a letter
   alignment of the anchor against the closest form; "a val" against "eval" aligns `a`→`e`, so it is part of the term.
   "Parse the config" → "parse config" still reverts (`the` aligns with nothing).
2. **`inflection` skipped** when the biased hit words are exactly one of the term's forms **and the biased word is the
   longer one** (the bias added the suffix: "LAN" → "land"). Deviation from the brief's "never": without the second
   condition "he parsed args" → "he parse args" (the documented `parse`→`parsed` case, an existing test) would keep
   the stripped inflection, because "parse args" is also exactly a form of `parse_args`.
3. **`in_anchor` → new keep reason `anchor_case`** when the anchor's words equal a form only after normalization (case
   or symbols): the biased words stay ("model.Train" vs `model.train`). An anchor that spells the form exactly still
   reverts (syn-047 "localhost colon 8000").
4. **COMMAND term + subcommand**: a hit on a `TERM_KIND_COMMAND` term followed in the biased text by one of its
   subcommands (`symbols.SUBCOMMANDS`) does not revert on `anchor_confident`. Other rules still apply.
5. **`no_hit` → `caps_oov` revert**: one biased token, all caps, ≥ 2 letters, not in `common_words`, not a word of any
   shortlist form, where the anchor heard one `common_words` word. Measured below: it fires once in the 4 runs
   (syn-008), and in the 12 stored reports of this set (`eval-baseline/`, `eval-merged/`) it would have fired on
   syn-008 only. Net +1, no loss; taken, with thin evidence (see risks).

## Risks

- `caps_oov` rests on one clip. A real acronym that is no shortlist term and no common word ("LLM") misheard by the
  anchor as a dictionary word ("elm") would be reverted. Common acronyms that are dictionary entries (`us`, `ok`,
  `pc`, `id`) never trigger it.
- The subcommand cue trusts the biased text for the next word; "Git push" where the speaker said "get push" is the
  accepted trade.
